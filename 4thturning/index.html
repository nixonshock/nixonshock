Saeculum Atlas the Fourth Turning set against the record

The Fourth Turning proposes that history repeats in cycles of about 80–90 years, each split into four moods: High, Awakening, Unraveling and Crisis. This page lays that claim beside dated events from several regions and beside rival cycle theories, so you can see where the pattern holds and where it is stretched.

What this page can and cannot show

How to read the badges

  • S&H claim: a date or label that comes from Strauss and Howe's own tables and books. It is their reading, not a measurement.
  • Documented fact: a dated event in standard histories.
  • Contested: a cycle whose boundaries differ between authors, or a theory applied beyond its authors' scope.
  • Forecast: a prediction that has not resolved.

Known criticism

  • Boundaries are drawn after the events are known, so the pattern can bend to fit. Peter Turchin calls this the Procrustean method.
  • Francis Fukuyama, reviewing the 2023 update, called the turning points arbitrary.
  • The theory covers Anglo-American history. Applying it to other regions is an extension its authors did not make in the same detail.
  • The proposed mechanism, cycles in parenting style, has little macro-level evidence.

Not in this version

  • Indicator charts (public debt, inequality, institutional trust, political violence). They need cleaned time series per region.
  • A link to a source on every event. Event dates here follow standard reference histories and should be checked before they are published as fact.
  • Modelski's long cycles and Ibn Khaldun's cycle as further lenses.
  • Turning dates before 1594 and the 1594–1704 sub-turnings follow the Wikipedia table and were not re-verified against a second source. They carry a note in the detail card.
